## Qualification and dedup decisions

The three Chemistry/Physics/Electronics rows repeat a six-credit list containing overlapping science subjects. Store one named common-scope clarification, not three copies or an invented substitution rule. Programme records retain their distinct diploma majors/classifications and placement. Geography's Year2 IGSCE wording and Geology's first-year science grouping are `unknown`. Geography's six school credits and Statistics' six A-level points across two principal subjects are different measures. Geology's second-year paragraph explicitly names five A-level subjects at C; it is not silently reduced to a conventional three-subject offer. Points tables stay source-specific; no IB/AP conversion is manufactured.

Actuarial eligibility groups are conditional rather than four simultaneous compulsory qualifications. The65% condition concerns mathematics subjects, not an overall degree average or CFA examination score. Financial mathematics is one of at least two actuarial-board subjects. Professional alternatives and the generic diploma/post-diploma-work baseline do not explicitly fit together; that uncertainty is recorded once in the common scope, together with the other non-diploma alternatives.

Music's BMUS code matches the earlier economic call's UNIMA BMUS despite Bachelor of Music versus Bachelor of Arts in Music wording, so the existing programme is reused. Actuarial Science/Sciences is a singular/plural variant of the existing honours award. In contrast, Family and Consumer Science/BSCFCS is not declared identical to the earlier Family Sciences/UNIMA-SCIFC. Its full named requirements remain visible in a common undergraduate scope without a speculative second award or false binding.

The two diplomas are not modelled as bachelor's programmes. Their individually named conditions share an other-level, other-entry scope; no formal framework level is inferred. The Statistics diploma sentence joins certificate/work and school-credit holders with `and`, without clearly establishing a cumulative or alternative relationship. That ambiguity remains unknown. This is a known catalogue limitation: the two diploma awards do not yet have separate programme selectors, but their requirements are present and named in the other-level selector.

Music's four OR-separated routes are preserved with their respective school-credit, theory-grade, practice and audition/portfolio conditions. The six-credit Grade5 option does not itself repeat practice/audition wording; the generic baseline is not silently waived or extended. The three Diploma in Music options are distinct; the practical-experience option requires an audition and does not publish a portfolio substitute. Fine Arts' unrelated-diploma route requires four arts-work years plus original-work portfolio and Year2 commencement; these conditions are not extended to its art-diploma alternative. Management bachelor's degrees are added advantages, not required second degrees; law-enforcement experience is agency-specific.
